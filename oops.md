EXACT PROCEDURES TBD: A. Transport Hardware To Site 
Start: The engine hardware will be assembled in the main assembly area, checked and sent to the site.
End: The engine hardware arrives on site.
As much of the tech dev hardware will be assembled before transportation to site. The combustion chamber, oxidizer tank, thrust cage and as much of the oxidizer tank mount as practical will be assembled before arriving on site. Tests of instrumentation, controls and hydro testing will be performed at UVic before competition. Then, components that must maintain oxygen cleanliness will be cleaned at UVic and shipped in contaminant free bags and with covers.

B. Engine Setup on Site
Start: Tech dev hardware arrives on site.
End: Tech dev setup finished, energetics armed.
We will work with competition organizers to find a spot in the Tech Dev area that is compatible with our site plan for engine hardware. At this location, we will rigidly fix our thrust cage and oxidizer tank mount to the ground with stakes. In parallel, mission control will be set up out of the safety clear zone. 
The oxidizer tank will have all of its ox clean covers removed and be mounted to its stand. The fill umbilical will be connected. With LC permission and following range safety, Cold flow leak tests using Nitrous Oxide will be performed between mission control and the assembled feed system. Upon successfully completing system checks, the team will proceed with the hotfire procedure:
All non critical personnel will leave the site’s safety critical zone except the Operations Lead and the Safety Captain, who will arm the pyrotechnic energetic devices.

C. Oxidizer Fill
Start: Safety Captain and Operations Lead actuate the solenoid to lock QD.
End: Confirm NC Fill Valve is closed.

At mission control, the valve positions will be confirmed and actuated to match the planned starting positions where required. The air compressor which provides compressed air to the QD solenoid will be turned on. The oxidizer fill stage is performed by the Safety Captain and Operations Lead, who will first actuate the solenoid and confirm the QD is locked. Upon confirmation, the NOS integrated cylinder valve will be slowly opened, pressurizing the line up until the NC Fill Valve. 

From there, the Safety Captain and Operations Lead will return to mission control with the “lock out” key. Then, from mission control the dump valve will be closed, the NC fill valve will be opened which will allow nitrous to fill the tank and evaporate out of the vent valve. When mission control reads the load cell weight which shows there is enough oxidizer mass as defined by the engine envelope, the vent valve and NC fill valve will be closed.

D. Fill Port Retracts
Start: Unactuate solenoid to unlock and retract QD
End: Mission Control Visually Confirms QD Disconnect Successful.

The solenoid is unactuated to unlock the QD, which will retract itself with a spring. A check valve in the oxidizer tank prevents oxidizer backflow. Via a remote video feed, mission control will verify the QD successfully disconnected. To respect DTEG R2.2.65 this will occur within the 30-count of the launch sequence. 

E. Activate Ignitier
Start: Mission control sends engine ignition command.
End: Engine operator must visually confirm igniter smoke.
The signal to ignite the engine will be sent from mission control, this is a hazardous command. As per LC R2.3.8, mission control must confirm seeing the igniter’s black smoke before sending command to open MEV.
F. Main Engine Valve Opens
Start: Signal sent to MEV black powder e-match. 
End: E-match ignites black powder, moving the main engine valve to its open position
The engine will ignite upon opening the MEV.
G. Engine Fires
Start: Nitrous oxide flows into the combustion chamber, and the heat from the igniter and melted fuel react to create steady combustion.
End: The liquid fully drains from the tank, and the vapor drains after it. The MEV will close when the tank pressure drops below a certain level, causing the engine to run out of oxidizer and the combustion to cease.

H. Test Stand Shutdown 
Start: Visual confirmation that engine flame has extinguished
End: All hardware ready to load into vehicle and depart site

Last, once the engine is confirmed to be shutdown visually, the system will be vented. Safety critical telemetry will be monitored to confirm the system has depressurized. We will communicate with LC staff to determine a safe time to enter the tech dev area and begin takedown.

The Safety Captain and Operations Lead will then enter the safety clear site to close the integrated cylinder valve. Operations Lead will bring the lockout key. Once this is closed, there will still be pressurized gas between the cylinder and the NC Fill Valve. The flex hose on the QD line will be mechanically restrained from flailing around. Next, both leads will return to mission control with the lockout key and actuate the NC Fill Valve and unactuate the solenoid to completely depressurize the system. The safe zone is now clear for all personnel to approach. Disassembly technicians must wear respirators and disassemble the engine downwind because of ash in the chamber.
